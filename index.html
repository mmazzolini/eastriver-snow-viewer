About the data

Companion to the paper on assimilating sparse ICESat-2 (IC2) snow depth with Gaussian-process (GP) spatial propagation in the East River basin, Colorado. The figure compares the experiments with Airborne Snow Observatory (ASO) lidar on 1 April 2023 and 3 April 2024.

Method in brief

  • Model. ERA5 downscaled with TopoPyScale drives FSM2 in 5400 GP-selected cells plus 400 IC2 representative cells; the ensembles are mapped to the 50 m grid with the GP predictive mean.
  • Observations. Synthetic IC2 snow depth: the 3 m ASO map sampled along real ICESat-2 tracks (median per 50 m cell), with a slope-dependent error SD of 0.40, 0.89 and 1.48 m (slopes below 10°, 10–40°, above 40°), reduced by about 5 %.
  • Assimilation. 100 members, DES-MDA with 4 iterations over the water year. Precipitation (multiplicative, median 1) and temperature (additive) perturbations are spatially correlated through ρ; the update is localised with ρ² and ignores observations with ρ < 0.37.
  • Experiments. GPE: ρ is the Matérn 5/2 kernel trained on the 2022 ASO map. GCE: ρ is the Gaspari–Cohn function (c = 5) on the same features.
  • Scores. Bias and RMSE of the ensemble mean, CRPS of the ensemble, and CRPS skill = 1 − CRPSpost / CRPSprior, all against ASO.

Reading the figure

Profile steps are means over the profile cells; bands are the mean of the per-cell percentiles. A dragged segment gives each member's mean over the cells between its two lines. Gridded products are single values on their own grid (each 50 m cell takes the product cell around it), so their CRPS is the mean absolute error. None of them uses ICESat-2.
